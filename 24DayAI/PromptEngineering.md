# Lesson 1: Prompt Engineering Basics
**Duration:** 2–2.5 hours | **Level:** Beginner | **Prerequisite:** None (just a free ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini account)

## Learning Objective
By the end of this session, students will be able to write clear, structured prompts that consistently produce useful AI output — instead of vague, disappointing answers.

---

## 1. Why This Is the #1 Skill to Learn First (10 min)

Open with this line: *"The AI didn't get smarter overnight — you just learned how to talk to it."*

Key point to land: AI doesn't read your mind. It responds to exactly what you type. Most "bad AI answers" people complain about are actually bad prompts, not bad AI.

**Quick demo (do this live):**
Ask the AI: `Write about marketing`
Then ask: `Write a 100-word Instagram caption for a small bakery's new sourdough loaf, in a warm and playful tone`

Show the room the difference. This contrast is the entire lesson in one demo.

---

## 2. The CTFT Framework (30 min)

Teach this as a simple, memorable formula:

| Element | What It Means | Question to Ask Yourself |
|---|---|---|
| **C – Context** | Background info the AI needs | Who is this for? What's the situation? |
| **T – Task** | The specific action you want | What exactly should the AI do? |
| **F – Format** | How you want the output structured | List? Table? Email? Word count? |
| **T – Tone** | The voice/style of the response | Formal? Casual? Persuasive? Simple? |

### Weak vs. Strong Prompt Examples

**Example 1 — Business**
- ❌ Weak: `Help me with my resume`
- ✅ Strong: `I'm applying for a marketing coordinator role at a mid-size retail company. Here's my current resume [paste]. Rewrite my "Experience" section to highlight leadership and data-driven results. Keep it to bullet points, 4th grade average sentence length, and a confident, professional tone.`

**Example 2 — Everyday Use**
- ❌ Weak: `Give me a recipe`
- ✅ Strong: `Give me a 30-minute dinner recipe using chicken, spinach, and rice. I'm cooking for 2 people, one of us doesn't eat spicy food. Format it as ingredients list + numbered steps.`

**Example 3 — Learning**
- ❌ Weak: `Explain machine learning`
- ✅ Strong: `Explain machine learning to a complete beginner using a real-world analogy (like teaching a child), in under 150 words, no technical jargon.`

**Teaching tip:** Have students say out loud which part of CTFT was missing in each "weak" example before you reveal the fix.

---

## 3. Hands-On Exercise (30 min)

Give each student this worksheet. They do it live on their own device.

**Step 1 — Diagnose:** Here are 3 weak prompts. Identify which CTFT elements are missing.
1. `Write me an email`
2. `Make a workout plan`
3. `Summarize this document`

**Step 2 — Rewrite:** Turn each into a strong prompt using CTFT.

**Step 3 — Test It:** Run both the weak and strong version in the AI tool and compare results side-by-side.

**Step 4 — Share:** 3–4 volunteers read their before/after to the group.

---

## 4. Common Beginner Mistakes (15 min)

Go through these quickly with examples:

1. **Being too vague** — "make it better" (better how? for whom?)
2. **Assuming the AI remembers unstated context** — always restate key details, especially in a new chat
3. **One-shot expectations** — treat it like a conversation; refine with follow-ups ("make it shorter," "more formal," "add a joke")
4. **Overloading one prompt** — break big asks into steps instead of cramming 5 requests into one
5. **Not specifying format** — if you don't ask for a table, you might get a wall of text

---

## 5. The Follow-Up Habit (10 min)

Teach that prompting is iterative, not one-and-done. Show this pattern:

```
Prompt 1: [Initial request]
Prompt 2: "Make it more concise"
Prompt 3: "Now write it in a friendlier tone"
Prompt 4: "Turn this into 3 bullet points"
```

Emphasize: **refining beats perfecting the first prompt.** This removes the pressure beginners feel to "get it right" immediately.

---

## 6. Practice Challenge (20 min)

Students pick ONE real task from their own life or work (an email, a plan, a explanation they need) and:
1. Write a first weak-ish attempt
2. Apply CTFT to strengthen it
3. Run it, then do 2 rounds of follow-up refinement
4. Share the final result with a partner

---

## 7. Recap & Cheat Sheet (5 min)

Give students this takeaway card:

> **Before you hit enter, ask:**
> - Have I given **Context**? (who/what/why)
> - Is my **Task** specific and clear?
> - Did I specify the **Format**? (length, structure, style)
> - Did I set the **Tone**? (formal, casual, persuasive...)
> - Am I ready to **follow up and refine**, not expect perfection first try?

---

## Homework / Between-Session Practice
Write 5 CTFT-based prompts for 5 different real tasks in your life (work, personal, learning). Bring your favorite one to share next session.

## Instructor Notes
- Keep the live demo genuinely simple — the "aha" moment comes from a stark before/after, not a complicated example.
- Expect some students to write prompts that are too long and rambling in the opposite direction — reinforce that CTFT is about clarity, not length.
- If time allows, show one "bad" AI answer caused by a vague prompt and let the class diagnose what's missing — this reinforces the lesson from Day 1's earlier general demo.
