# Lesson 2: Using ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini Fluently
**Duration:** 2.5–3 hours | **Level:** Beginner | **Prerequisite:** Lesson 1 (Prompt Engineering Basics)

## Learning Objective
By the end of this session, students will know each major AI platform's unique features, when to reach for which tool, and how to move beyond "just chatting" into using follow-ups, file uploads, voice, and model-switching like a power user.

*(Note for instructor: AI products change fast — features and model names shift every few months. Treat exact names below as "current as of this writing" and have students verify inside the apps on the day of class.)*

---

## 1. The Big Picture: Same Job, Three Different Personalities (10 min)

Analogy to open with: *"ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are like three highly skilled consultants. They can all write, reason, and analyze — but they were trained by different companies with different priorities, so they each have a distinct 'personality' and toolkit."*

- **ChatGPT (OpenAI)** — the generalist with the biggest ecosystem
- **Claude (Anthropic)** — the careful writer/analyst/coder
- **Gemini (Google)** — the multimodal, Google-integrated researcher

---

## 2. Platform-by-Platform Deep Dive

### 🟢 ChatGPT (OpenAI)

**Unique features:**
- **Custom GPTs** — build your own mini-AI-app with custom instructions and knowledge, no coding (e.g., a "Resume Coach GPT")
- **GPT Store** — browse and use thousands of Custom GPTs made by others
- **Voice Mode** — natural, low-latency spoken conversation, useful for practicing languages or hands-free brainstorming
- **Advanced Data Analysis (Code Interpreter)** — upload a spreadsheet and it writes/runs code behind the scenes to analyze it and build charts
- **DALL·E image generation** — built directly into chat
- **Plugins/Actions & broad third-party integrations** — connects to many external tools and services
- **Memory** — remembers facts about you across conversations (can be turned off)

**Strengths:** Most versatile all-in-one tool; huge ecosystem; best for people who want ONE app to do writing, images, data, and voice.
**Weaknesses:** Can be "eager to please" — sometimes overconfident or overly agreeable rather than critical; quality can vary across the many Custom GPTs.
**Most apt for:** Everyday general use, brainstorming, voice practice, quick image generation, anyone who wants breadth over depth in one subscription.

---

### 🟣 Claude (Anthropic)

**Unique features:**
- **Projects** — a persistent workspace with shared instructions and uploaded documents/files that stays consistent across many conversations (great for ongoing work like a course, a book, or a client account)
- **Artifacts** — generates documents, code, slides, or interactive mini-apps in a separate window you can edit and reuse, instead of burying them in chat text
- **Extended/Deep thinking mode** — the model visibly reasons step-by-step through hard problems before answering, useful for legal, financial, or multi-constraint reasoning
- **Large context window** — can hold very long documents/conversations in "memory" at once (useful for analyzing long contracts, books, or codebases)
- **Strong file and document handling** — reads PDFs, spreadsheets, and long reports with high accuracy

**Strengths:** Widely regarded as the strongest for writing quality, careful reasoning, coding, and working with long documents; tends to be more direct and less likely to "make things up" (hallucinate) than competitors.
**Weaknesses:** Smaller built-in ecosystem — no native image generation, fewer third-party plugins, no native audio/video input, less tightly wired into other apps out of the box.
**Most apt for:** Long-form writing and editing, coding, analyzing long documents/contracts, careful step-by-step reasoning, and any task where accuracy matters more than flash.

---

### 🔵 Gemini (Google)

**Unique features:**
- **Deep Research** — can run extended, multi-step research tasks that pull information from the web and compile it into a structured report, ideal for competitive or market research
- **Native multimodality** — handles text, images, audio, and video input in one model more seamlessly than the others
- **Google Workspace integration** — works directly inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets, and Slides
- **Massive context window** — can process extremely long documents or even entire codebases/videos at once
- **Free tier is generous**, and often bundled into existing Google accounts/Workspace subscriptions

**Strengths:** Best choice if your life/work already runs on Google tools; excels at multimodal tasks (e.g., analyzing a video or image alongside text) and large-scale research synthesis.
**Weaknesses:** Writing quality and nuanced reasoning are generally seen as a step behind Claude; less polished "personality" in casual conversation compared to the other two.
**Most apt for:** Google Workspace users, multimodal tasks (video/image + text together), large research projects, students who want a strong free tier.

## 4. Beyond Basic Chat: Fluency Skills (30 min)

Walk through each of these live, one platform at a time:

1. **Follow-ups** — Show that AI conversations are threads, not one-shot Q&A. Demo: ask for a paragraph, then say "make it shorter," then "now translate it to Spanish."
2. **Editing AI's response directly** — Some platforms let you click into and edit the output, or ask it to revise just one section.
3. **Uploading files** — Have every student upload one real file (a PDF, resume, or spreadsheet) and ask a question about it.
4. **Voice mode** — Demo asking a question out loud and getting a spoken answer back (ChatGPT and Gemini). Discuss when this beats typing (multitasking, practicing a language, brainstorming while walking).
5. **Switching models mid-conversation** — Show students where the model picker lives in each app, and that choosing a "faster/lighter" vs. "smarter/slower" model is a real, useful choice (see next section).

---

## 5. Understanding "Which Model" — The Advanced Model Difference (25 min)

Explain with this analogy: *"Inside each app, you're often choosing between a fast, efficient assistant for quick tasks and a slower, more capable expert for hard tasks. Bigger/slower ≠ always better — it depends on the job."*

| Platform | "Fast/Everyday" tier | "Advanced/Deep Reasoning" tier | What actually differs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT** | Smaller/faster model, quick answers | Larger flagship model, better reasoning, longer context, higher usage limits on paid plans | Speed vs. depth of reasoning; paid tiers unlock the strongest models plus higher message limits |
| **Claude** | Smaller/faster tier (e.g., a "Haiku"-class model) | Largest, most capable tier (e.g., an "Opus"-class model), with an extended-thinking mode for step-by-step reasoning | The advanced tier reasons more carefully and handles more complex, high-stakes tasks, but is slower and costs more per use |
| **Gemini** | "Flash" tier — very fast, cheaper, good for high-volume simple tasks | "Pro" tier — stronger reasoning, larger context window, better for research and complex synthesis | Flash trades some depth for speed and cost; Pro is worth it for anything that needs careful analysis |

**Teaching point:** In every platform, the pattern is the same — a **fast/cheap tier** for simple, high-volume tasks (drafting a quick reply, summarizing a short email) and a **flagship/advanced tier** for anything that requires deep reasoning, long documents, or high accuracy (legal review, complex coding, strategic analysis). Beginners should get comfortable manually switching to the advanced tier when a task actually matters.

---

## 6. Hands-On Exercise (30 min)

Give each student the **same real task** to run across all three platforms (e.g., *"Summarize this 2-page article into 5 bullet points and suggest one counter-argument"*):

1. Run it in ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini
2. Compare: Which felt clearest? Which was fastest? Which handled the file best?
3. Try the follow-up: "Now make it more skeptical/critical"
4. Fill in a personal "which tool, which job" cheat sheet based on their own experience, not just this lesson

---

## 7. Recap & Takeaway Card

> **Quick decision guide:**
> - Need an image, voice chat, or one all-purpose app? → **ChatGPT**
> - Writing, editing, coding, or a long document that must be accurate? → **Claude**
> - Live inside Gmail/Docs/Sheets, or working with video/large research? → **Gemini**
> - Task is quick and simple? → Use the **fast/lite model**
> - Task is complex, high-stakes, or long? → Switch to the **advanced/flagship model**

## Homework
Pick one task you do weekly (an email, a report, a plan). Run it through all three platforms once, and write 2–3 sentences on which one you'd keep using for that specific task and why.

## Instructor Notes
- Model names and features change every few months — before teaching this lesson, spend 10 minutes checking each platform's official site/changelog for anything renamed or newly added.
- Avoid declaring one platform "the best" — the goal is matching tool to task, not brand loyalty.
- If budget is a concern for students, spend extra time on each platform's free tier limits so they know what's realistic without paying.
