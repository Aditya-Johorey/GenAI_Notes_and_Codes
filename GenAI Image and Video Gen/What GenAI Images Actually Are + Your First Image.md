# Part 1 — What is Generative AI? (5 Minutes)

## Traditional Computer Programs

Normally computers follow instructions.

For example:

```
IF user clicks button
    Open file
```

Or

```
Draw a red square
```

Everything is explicitly programmed.

---

## Generative AI Works Differently

Instead of following step-by-step drawing instructions,

the AI has learned patterns from **billions of images and their captions** during training.

When you write

> A golden retriever playing football

the AI isn't searching Google for that image.

It is **creating a completely new picture** based on everything it has learned about:

* dogs
* footballs
* grass
* movement
* sunlight
* camera angles
* anatomy
* colors

The final image has never existed before.

---

## The Chef Analogy

Imagine teaching a chef by showing them:

* 10 million cakes
* every type of frosting
* every decorating style
* every ingredient

Now ask:

> Make me a chocolate cake with blue frosting shaped like a castle.

The chef doesn't copy another cake.

The chef combines learned knowledge into something new.

Image AI works similarly.

# What Happens Behind the Scenes?

Very simplified process:

```
Your Prompt

↓

AI understands the meaning

↓

Breaks the prompt into concepts

↓

Predicts what should appear

↓

Creates pixels

↓

Produces the final image
```

Notice something important.

It never searches for one matching picture.

It generates one.

---

# Common Myth

### Myth

AI copies images from Google.

### Reality

Modern image models generate new pixel arrangements.

They have learned visual patterns rather than storing a library of finished pictures.

Like a human artist, they may produce work influenced by what they learned, but they synthesize rather than retrieve images.

---

# Text → Pixels

Suppose we type

> A futuristic city at sunset.

The AI understands concepts such as

```
City

↓

Buildings

↓

Glass

↓

Roads

↓

Sky

↓

Orange sunset

↓

Lighting

↓

Perspective

↓

Reflection

↓

Pixels
```

Every pixel is predicted based on surrounding pixels until the image gradually appears.

# Part 2 — Anatomy of a Good Prompt (10 Minutes)

Most beginners write prompts like this:

> cat

or

> dragon

or

> mountain

That gives the AI very little information.

A good prompt answers five questions.

---

# The Five Building Blocks

```
Subject

+

Style

+

Setting

+

Lighting / Mood

+

Extra Details
```

Let's explore each one.

---

# 1. Subject

The main thing you want.

Examples

* astronaut
* lion
* robot
* woman
* coffee shop
* spaceship

Examples

❌ Bird

✅ A colorful macaw parrot

---

# 2. Style

How should it look?

Examples

* realistic
* watercolor
* Pixar
* anime
* oil painting
* cyberpunk
* fantasy
* sketch
* low poly
* pixel art
* cinematic
* digital painting

Example

Instead of

> Cat

Try

> A realistic cat

or

> An anime cat

---

# 3. Setting

Where?

Examples

* rainforest
* Mars
* Tokyo street
* medieval castle
* beach
* mountain
* underwater
* library
* futuristic city

---

# 4. Lighting / Mood

Lighting changes everything.

Examples

* sunrise
* golden hour
* rainy
* neon lights
* moonlight
* dramatic shadows
* soft lighting
* cozy
* mysterious
* horror
* dreamy

---

# 5. Extra Details

This separates average images from amazing ones.

Examples

* shallow depth of field
* ultra detailed
* 8K
* cinematic composition
* intricate textures
* volumetric lighting
* reflections
* smoke
* rain droplets
* vibrant colors

---

# Prompt Formula

```
Subject

+

Style

+

Setting

+

Lighting

+

Details
```

---

# Example

Weak Prompt

```
Robot
```

Better

```
A futuristic humanoid robot standing inside a modern laboratory.
```

Even Better

```
A futuristic humanoid robot standing inside a clean high-tech laboratory, cinematic lighting, realistic metal textures, blue glowing eyes, shallow depth of field, ultra detailed, photorealistic.
```

# Prompt Iteration

Don't stop after the first result.

Instead, improve it.

Example

> Mountain

↓

> Snow-covered mountain at sunrise

Prompting is an iterative process.

# Common Beginner Mistakes

❌ Writing only one or two words.

❌ Trying to describe everything at once.

❌ Expecting the first result to be perfect.

❌ Forgetting lighting.

❌ Forgetting the environment.

❌ Ignoring artistic style.

❌ Using contradictory instructions (e.g., "night scene in bright midday sunlight").

---

# Prompt Improvement Checklist

Before generating an image, ask yourself:

* What is the main subject?
* Which artistic style do I want?
* Where is it located?
* What mood or lighting fits the scene?
* What extra details will make it unique?

If you can answer all five, your prompt is usually strong.

# Key Takeaways

* AI image generators create new images by learning visual patterns, not by searching for existing pictures.
* The quality of the output depends heavily on the quality of the prompt.
* A strong prompt follows the formula:
  **Subject + Style + Setting + Lighting/Mood + Details**.
* Image generation is an iterative process—refine your prompt based on each result.
* **Habit to build:** Write prompts as clear, descriptive sentences rather than a list of disconnected keywords. This gives the AI more context and typically leads to better, more coherent images.
